Two packages.
One tenant spine.

Individual and Business should not be two products. Both are workspaces; a role belongs to a membership, not to a person. That one decision makes Google and Microsoft identity, team roles, approvals and billing fall into place without forking the codebase.

1workspace model
4roles per membership
2identity providers + email
nworkspaces per person

Core decision

An Individual account is a PERSONAL workspace with one member. A Business account is a BUSINESS workspace with N members. Same schema, same guards — different entitlements.

Consequences: a freelancer can also be an employee elsewhere; switching companies never mixes data; seat billing is a count, not a special case; “HR / Manager / Employee” is workspace-scoped authorization.

01

Two packages, one shape

The choice at sign-up only sets the workspace type and plan. Everything else — authentication, membership, documents, notifications, audit — is shared.

Individual

For a solo professional

A one-seat workspace where the member is the owner. Personal record-keeping, not people management.

  • Personal clock-in, timesheet and work-hour insights
  • Personal leave and absence calendar
  • Payslip and document vault, self-uploaded
  • Reminders, exports and local notifications
  • No approvals, payroll administration, geofence admin or SSO
Workspace PERSONAL Plan INDIVIDUAL_* · 1 seat
Business

For a company and its team

The owner creates the company profile, invites members, assigns roles and reporting lines, and connects the company’s identity provider.

  • Team directory, org chart and reporting lines
  • Roles: owner, HR admin, manager, employee
  • Leave approvals, attendance, branches and geofences
  • Payroll, GOSI and Mudad/WPS exports on higher plans
  • Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 SSO, group mapping, audit
Workspace BUSINESS Plans STARTER → ENTERPRISE
→ Upgrade is a conversion, not a migration. A personal workspace becomes a business workspace in place, keeping its data. A business can only return to personal when one member remains.

04

Google or Microsoft, per company

Authentication answers “who are you”; workspace authorization answers “what can you do here”. SSO is a workspace connection, not a global app setting.

1

Enter the work email

The client calls POST /auth/discover. The response includes the matching workspaces and whether SSO is enforced for that domain.

2

Branch on the workspace policy

No enforced SSOPassword or social sign-in remains available. Existing memberships are listed after verification.
Enforced SSORedirect to Google Workspace or Microsoft Entra. Password login is refused for that workspace.
3

Verify the provider proof

Google: require hd to equal the configured domain. Microsoft: require tid to equal the configured tenant. Mismatch is rejected before any membership lookup.

4

Resolve the membership

ExistingLoad role and reporting line from Membership; issue a workspace-scoped token.
Missing + JIT offReturn 403 INVITE_REQUIRED. Never auto-create a member without consent.
5

Provision or map roles

When JIT is on and the domain/tenant is proven, create the user and membership with defaultRole. Apply group mapping (for example managers@ → Manager). HR mapping needs an explicit owner opt-in.

6

Choose the workspace

One membership enters directly. Several memberships show a picker. The token carries workspaceId, membershipId, role and a session version.

7

Guard every request

The API revalidates that the membership is active, belongs to the claimed workspace, and holds the required permission and plan entitlement. A workspace ID in a request body is never trusted.

08

How to get there from the current repo

Schema first, guards second, surfaces third. The existing Macquires data becomes the first business workspace; personal sign-ups create one-seat workspaces.

Phase 0

Design sign-off

Accept the five decisions, confirm individual scope, approval default and salary visibility.

Phase 1

Schema and backfill

Workspace, Membership, Invite, AuthIdentity, SSO, PayrollRun. Add workspaceId to domain tables; composite uniques; backfill Macquires.

Phase 2

Auth and guards

Workspace-scoped JWT, discovery, invite accept, switch endpoint, permission guard. Google adapter added; Entra verification reused.

Phase 3

Mobile surfaces

Package onboarding, workspace switcher, real membership role replacing the client-side switcher, personal mode hides org modules.

Phase 4

Admin console

Team table, role and reporting-line editing, invites, SSO settings, group mapping, seat meter, plan page.

Phase 5

Commercialization

Billing webhooks, entitlement guards, directory sync, audit export, rate limits, retention policies.

! Blockers to fix while the schema is open: unguarded GET /employees/:id exposing salary and national ID; single-stage leave approvals; UTC date bucketing for Riyadh/Pakistan punches; duplicate reportsToId and radiusMeters fields; shared default password.
09

Decisions still open

These change the build order or the commercial model. Everything else is specified in the README.

  1. Individual scope. Is it free, freemium, or paid? Does it include personal payroll tracking or only time, leave and documents?
  2. Ownership. One owner per workspace, or co-owners? Recommendation: one owner with explicit transfer.
  3. Manager model. Role plus reporting line (recommended), or derive manager purely from direct reports?
  4. Approval default. MANAGER_ONLY or MANAGER_THEN_HR for new businesses?
  5. Salary visibility. Can the owner see individual salaries, or only HR and aggregate totals?
  6. Directory sync. Ship Google/Microsoft group sync in v1, or SSO plus manual invites first?
  7. One identity provider per workspace. Restrict to one for clarity, or allow Google identity with Microsoft Teams notifications (recommended)?
